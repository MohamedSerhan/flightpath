# 03 - Competitive Scan: Pilot Job-Search Apps & Sites

**Date:** 2026-05-07
**Question:** Where can a CFI-focused, freshness-first, free-to-pilot app actually win?

---

## 1. Aggregator Websites

### Climbto350
- **One-liner:** Long-running aviation job aggregator covering pilots, mechanics, dispatch, and management roles.
- **Audience:** All seats — heavily skewed toward 121/135/corporate. Light coverage of CFI / sub-1500-hour roles.
- **Pricing:** Pilots pay $14.95/mo or ~$69/yr. Employers post for free with unlimited resume-database access.
- **Strengths:** Largest brand recognition in the segment; updated frequently; mobile app exists (4.9 stars, ~265 ratings).
- **Weaknesses:** Heavy criticism on r/flying, Jetcareers, PPRuNe, Flightinfo for (a) scraping listings from other sites and Facebook (one user said 80–90% of listings were also on Facebook with identical text), (b) zombie listings — Everts Air kept reappearing despite no actual hiring, (c) almost no value for low-time pilots — one Road to 1500 audit of 470 March listings turned up exactly **one** sub-1500-hour role. Pilots are paying for an aggregator of jobs they could find free elsewhere.

### JSfirm
- **One-liner:** Broad aviation job board (pilot, A&P, dispatch, corporate).
- **Audience:** Mid-career and up; corporate, regional, 121.
- **Pricing:** Free for pilots. Employers pay from $164/single posting.
- **Strengths:** Free for job seekers. Large employer network. Unique FAA-database resume search for employers.
- **Weaknesses:** Filtering is keyword-heavy and dated; no hours-based filter; no salary transparency; CFI listings are sparse and often from large chains (ATP, American Flyers) only.

### Pilot Career Centre (PCC)
- **One-liner:** Canadian-rooted global pilot job navigator with strong regional pages.
- **Audience:** International — strong in Canada, USA, Europe, MENA, Asia, Oceania.
- **Pricing:** Free for pilots.
- **Strengths:** Best-in-class filtering by aircraft category (SEP, SET, MEP, MET) and by company/callsign. Has a dedicated "New Pilots" section for low-time roles, which is rare.
- **Weaknesses:** UI looks like 2008. Listings skew toward Canadian operators. No native mobile app. No notification system worth using.

### FltOps.com
- **One-liner:** Older subscription-style info service for professional pilots, plus job fairs.
- **Audience:** Established 121/corporate pilots.
- **Pricing:** Subscription (legacy model).
- **Strengths:** Established brand for job-fair access.
- **Weaknesses:** Forum threads on Airline Pilot Central question whether it's still actively maintained for postings. Not relevant to CFIs at all.

### BizJetJobs
- **One-liner:** Corporate and contract pilot job board, also acts as a staffing agency.
- **Audience:** Corporate / Part 91 / contract.
- **Pricing:** Member fee for pilots; employer fees.
- **Strengths:** Deep specialization in bizjet niche; hybrid job-board + staffing model creates real placements.
- **Weaknesses:** Zero relevance to CFIs and low-time pilots.

### FindAPilot
- **One-liner:** US pilot-employer matching with profile filtering.
- **Audience:** Mixed — leans regional/corporate.
- **Pricing:** Free profile creation; employer fees.
- **Strengths:** Filterable by position, aircraft type, type rating, location.
- **Weaknesses:** Modest listing volume; not first-stop for most pilots.

### PilotsGlobal
- **One-liner:** International pilot job network with employer reviews ("Glassdoor for pilots").
- **Audience:** Global airline pilots.
- **Pricing:** Freemium.
- **Strengths:** Employer reviews are genuinely useful and rare in this space.
- **Weaknesses:** Almost entirely 121/airline focus. CFI listings are nonexistent.

### Pilot Junction / JobsInAviation / Latest Pilot Jobs
- **One-liner:** Smaller aggregators, mostly international (Latest Pilot Jobs is UK-based).
- **Audience:** International airline.
- **Pricing:** Mostly free with email-alert upsells.
- **Strengths:** Email alerts.
- **Weaknesses:** Reviews call out crashes, broken filters, and stale data. Latest Pilot Jobs' app has explicit user reviews saying "wouldn't trust it" because the search criteria don't work.

### Indeed / LinkedIn / ZipRecruiter
- **The elephant in the room.** Indeed alone shows ~692 active CFI listings and ~886 broader Flight Instructor roles. ZipRecruiter has hundreds more. These are where flight schools and FBOs actually post for low-time pilots — because they post to Indeed by default for *every* job, not because they care about aviation niche.
- **Strengths:** Free to apply, real-time freshness, salary data shown, location filters work, push notifications.
- **Weaknesses:** No aircraft / hours / rating filters. CFI listings drown in a sea of "Computer Science Instructor" results unless you craft queries carefully. No community context.

## 2. Mobile Apps (App Store / Play Store)

| App | Rating | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **climbto350: aviation job board** | 4.9 / ~265 ratings | Most polished niche app. April 2026 update added push notifications. Same content limitations as the website. |
| **Latest Pilot Jobs** | Mixed / lower | Crash complaints, broken search, "no developer response." UK / international skew. |
| **PilotLink** | New, low review count | Map-search system; tries to let recruiters find pilots based on hours/ratings. Interesting model but unproven. |
| **MAP Aviation: Airlines Jobs** (Play Store) | Niche | Mostly international, low engagement. |
| **Indeed / LinkedIn apps** | Hundreds of thousands of ratings | Where the actual user volume is. |

**Takeaway:** No mobile app currently owns the CFI / sub-1500-hour segment with great UX, push notifications, hours/ratings filters, and freshness. Climbto350 is the closest, and pilots are openly skeptical of its low-time relevance.

## 3. Career-Tracking Tools That Pilots Already Live In

### LogTen Pro ($129/yr)
- **The most important competitor in this list.** LogTen has explicitly added a Careers feature: it matches your logbook hours, ratings, and experience against airline/Part 135/Part 91/corporate openings. It claims 121 airline jobs alongside corporate listings, all matched to *your* logbook. Used by 120,000+ pilots and integrated with major airline training departments.
- **Why this matters:** They own the data (your logbook) that any job-matching app needs. They're the only player with a real "match score" pipeline.
- **Weakness:** Career feature is airline/Part 135 focused — CFI roles are not their target. Annual paywall means hobby/student pilots aren't in.

### ForeFlight Logbook ($299/yr as part of subscription tiers)
- Best for ForeFlight users via auto-track. No job listings or career matching — purely logging.

### Pilot Records / Pilot Credentials / LiftAcademy
- Document storage and credential tracking. None surface jobs.

**Takeaway:** LogTen is the one to watch. They have the data moat. But they have not chosen to serve CFIs or the 250–1500 hour cohort with the careers feature.

## 4. The Real Pipeline: Newsletters, Discords, Reddit, Word of Mouth

Across r/flying, Jetcareers, Airline Pilot Forums, Pilots of America, Flightinfo, and PPRuNe, the same advice repeats almost verbatim:

> "Most good [CFI / low-time] jobs are filled by word of mouth or internal networking."
> "Walk into every FBO within driving distance with business cards."
> "Look in Facebook flying groups."
> "Cold-call flight schools where you'd want to live."

**Specific channels in active use:**
- **Reddit r/flying** — informal "where are you hiring" threads, no megathread.
- **Jetcareers forums** — "Jobs Available (Members Only)" section is one of the better-curated low-time pipelines.
- **Facebook groups** — region- and aircraft-specific flying groups carry many CFI postings that never hit job boards.
- **Discord servers** — multiple aviation/pilot Discords (Plane & Pilot, various student/CFI servers); used more for advice than for postings, but jobs leak through.
- **School-internal Slack / email lists** — ATP, American Flyers, Embry-Riddle, UND, Coast all hire largely from their own graduates first.
- **AskACFI / askacfi.com CFI Employment board** — small but legitimate.

**Key inference:** The CFI / low-time job market is structurally fragmented and informal. Listings are scattered across Facebook posts, Discord pings, FBO bulletin boards, and Indeed. *No one is aggregating this layer.* Climbto350 tries, but its scraping is shallow and biased toward 121/corporate.

## 5. Recruiters & Headhunters

### Aero Crew Solutions
- Pilot career consulting since 2010. Resume review, mock interviews, job-fair prep. Staff are former pilot recruiters.
- Pricing not public — quote-based. Not cheap.
- **Gap they fill:** Personalized prep that no board provides. Targets pilots applying to their first or next airline.

### FAPA (Future & Active Pilot Advisors)
- Career advisory + job fairs (frequent regional events with airlines like JetBlue).
- Membership: ~$389 initial + $29/mo for Pro, or older basic tier ~$49 + $4.95/mo.
- **Gap they fill:** Job fairs (face time with recruiters) and salary/hiring intel for the 121 path.
- **Weakness:** Expensive; airline-track only; no help for CFIs trying to land their first paid teaching job.

### Air Inc, Emerald Coast, etc.
- Similar, all targeting the airline track.

**Takeaway:** All recruiters serve pilots already past 1500 hours and aiming at majors. The 250–1500 hour cohort — student-CFIs, new CFIs, banner-tow, skydive-tow, traffic-watch — is unserved by paid consultants.

---

## Gap Analysis: Where a CFI-Focused, Freshness-First, Free App Wins

The honest summary: **the airline / 121 / corporate end of the market is over-served.** Climbto350, JSfirm, BizJetJobs, FAPA, Aero Crew Solutions, LogTen Careers, PilotsGlobal, and the airline ATS systems (airlineapps.com) all compete hard there. Adding another player at that end is a losing fight.

**But the CFI and sub-1500-hour cohort is genuinely underserved.** Concrete evidence:

1. **Paid boards skip them.** A 470-listing audit of Climbto350 found one low-time role. Climbto350's own users in r/flying call this out repeatedly.
2. **The structurally relevant listings are on Indeed, Facebook groups, Discord servers, school Slacks, and FBO bulletin boards** — fragmented and unsearchable as a unit.
3. **The published advice is "go cold-call FBOs"** — i.e., the community has accepted that no aggregator works for them. That is exactly the gap.
4. **No filtering by what actually matters at this stage:** TT, multi time, instrument given, MEI status, sponsorship willingness, housing offered, time-building rate, dual-given pay vs. salary structure. Indeed has none of this. Climbto350 has minimal hours filtering. PCC has aircraft category but not hours.
5. **No app pushes fresh CFI postings the moment they appear.** Climbto350 just added push notifications in late 2025 but the underlying content is still mostly 121/corporate.
6. **Salary transparency is absent across the board.** New CFIs have no idea whether $25/dual-hour or $45/hour is normal in their market. Glassdoor is not aviation-aware.
7. **The community-pipeline jobs (Discord/Facebook/school Slack) never reach a board.** A scraper-plus-community-submission model could capture them.

### The sharper niche

Not "another pilot job board." Specifically:

- **Audience:** 200–1500 hour pilots — student CFIs about to certificate, fresh CFIs/CFIIs/MEIs, banner-tow, traffic-watch, pipeline-patrol, and second-career pilots.
- **Promise:** Every CFI / time-building job in your driving radius, fresher than Indeed, filtered by what actually matters at this stage (hours required, dual-given pay structure, aircraft type, housing, sponsorship), with push notifications and salary data the rest of the market refuses to publish.
- **Moat:** (a) Structured scraping of Indeed + flight-school career pages + Facebook flying groups + Discord pinned channels. (b) Crowdsourced salary and "what it's actually like to work here" data from current CFIs. (c) Community submissions — the FBO bulletin-board layer digitized. (d) A real hours/ratings filter built around the FAA's actual experience categories, not a free-text keyword search.
- **Pricing:** Free for pilots, period. Monetize via flight-school employer postings (priced well below Climbto350-for-employers' free tier — i.e., charge for *featured* placement and applicant filtering, not for posting). The pilot-side audience is the asset; the employer side is where money comes from once volume exists.

### Where this could fail

Be honest:

- **LogTen could pivot down-market.** If they expanded their Careers feature to include CFI jobs and matched against the logbook of a student-CFI, they would have a structural advantage — they already own the hours data.
- **Indeed plus a saved search probably works "well enough"** for many users. The bar is freshness + filtering + community context, not just listings.
- **Word-of-mouth may resist productization.** Discord/Facebook job leakers may not want their jobs scraped; flight schools may prefer hiring their own graduates and never list publicly.
- **The 121 hiring slowdown** that has begun in 2025–2026 means the CFI pipeline is also softening — the timing is mixed. If ATP, American Flyers, et al. start absorbing every new CFI in-house, third-party listings shrink.

### Bottom line

The "general pilot job board" space is crowded, mature, and not winnable. The "CFI and time-builder" space is **structurally fragmented, informally networked, and explicitly unserved by every paid product on the market.** A free, mobile-first, freshness-obsessed app with real hours/ratings filtering and crowdsourced salary data has a credible wedge — provided it solves aggregation + freshness rather than trying to be Yet Another Portal.
